# Mission: Wine Knowledge (WSET Level 1 → Level 2)

## Why
Use wine knowledge professionally in food & beverage business and consulting work: build and
critique wine lists, brief staff, talk credibly with distributors and clients, and match wines to a
concept's menu and price points. Earn WSET credentials (Level 1, then the Level 2 Award in Wines)
as proof of that competence.

## Success looks like
- Describe any wine's structure (sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body) using the WSET Level 2
  Systematic Approach to Tasting (SAT) vocabulary, in front of a client or team.
- Name the 8 WSET Level 2 principal grape varieties, their typical styles, and where they're grown,
  and explain how climate and winemaking change style, quality and price.
- Read a wine label (region, variety, quality terms) and say what's likely in the bottle.
- Make defensible food-and-wine pairing calls for a menu.
- Pass WSET Level 2 Award in Wines (50 multiple-choice questions, 60 min, 55% pass), aiming for
  Merit (70%+) or better.

## Constraints
- Starting as a beginner: no formal wine training, no prior certification.
- Occasional tasting only (roughly a bottle or two a week, or the odd event), so lessons should get
  the most from each bottle and use home calibration exercises.
- Lessons happen in short sessions; state lives in git and must be pushed each session.

## Out of scope
- Court of Master Sommeliers path (service practical, blind deductive tasting) for now.
- WSET Level 3+ depth, spirits, sake and beer.
- Wine-business finance (list pricing, margins) until the Level 2 knowledge base is in place.
