Lesson 1 · Structure · WSET Levels 1–2

The five structural dials of every wine

Before grapes, regions or labels, learn the five things your mouth can measure in any glass.

Today's win

Name the five dials (sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body), say where each comes from, and feel each one in a single glass of red.

Every WSET Level 2 tasting note is built on the same palate scales.1 For your consulting work they're also the shared vocabulary for a wine list. "Light-bodied, high acidity, low tannin" tells a chef, an owner or a server what a wine will do at the table before anyone opens it. Grapes and regions come later, and you'll learn them as reasons a wine lands where it does on these dials.

The Level 2 exam is 50 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes. You need 55% to pass, 70% for Merit and 85% for Distinction.2

One sentence of winemaking first

Yeast eats the sugar in grape juice and turns it into alcohol and carbon dioxide. Sugar the yeast leaves behind is called residual sugar.3 Hold on to that. It's why sweetness and alcohol are linked: the riper the grapes and the further fermentation runs, the more alcohol and the less sugar end up in the wine.

Most wines you'll meet on a restaurant list are fermented dry, or close to it.

Two traps beginners fall into

Acidity versus tannin

Both feel "sharp", but they work in opposite directions. Acidity makes your mouth water. Tannin makes it dry.7 When you're unsure, swallow and wait: are you salivating, or does your mouth feel furry?

Fruity versus sweet

A dry wine with lots of ripe-fruit flavour often gets called "sweet". Sweetness is sugar on the tongue, and fruit is a flavour you smell. A guest who says a dry Sauvignon Blanc is "too sweet" usually means "too fruity". Knowing that keeps you from steering them toward the wrong bottle.

Recall it

Answer from memory, without scrolling up. Getting a question wrong now helps you remember it later.

You swallow a white wine and your mouth keeps watering for several seconds. Which dial is high?

Acidity makes you salivate, and the longer the watering lasts, the higher the acidity. Tannin does the opposite and dries the mouth.

Where does residual sugar come from?

It's the grape sugar the yeast didn't convert to alcohol. Stop fermentation early and you keep more sugar and make less alcohol.

A red leaves your gums and the inside of your cheeks feeling dry and grippy. Which dial is high?

That drying grip is tannin binding to the proteins in your saliva, the same feeling as over-steeped black tea.

Which of these contributes most to a wine's body?

Alcohol is the main source of weight, helped by sugar. A deep colour can look heavy, but colour has no weight in the mouth.

Tannin in red wine comes mainly from where?

Skins, seeds and stems, plus a little from oak. Red wines ferment with their skins, which is why they carry far more tannin than whites.

A client's guest calls a bone-dry, very fruity Sauvignon Blanc "sweet". What are they most likely tasting?

Fruit flavour gets mistaken for sweetness. A bone-dry wine has almost no sugar to taste.

Which is the WSET Level 2 sweetness scale, in order?

Dry, off-dry, medium, sweet. "Pronounced" belongs to flavour intensity, "brut" is a sparkling-wine label term, and "luscious" isn't on the Level 2 scale.

You need a red for a light lunch menu that won't dry diners' mouths out. Which dial should be low?

Tannin causes the drying. Low-tannin reds feel softer and easier at lunch.

Feel it: the one-bottle structure lab

This exercise comes from Wine Folly.6 You add one thing to each glass of the same red, so each glass changes mostly one dial and you can feel that dial on its own. It takes about 20 minutes and one inexpensive bottle.

Any cheap, dry red you'd happily drink will do. Avoid one that's already very tannic, because the change in glass A will be harder to feel.

  1. Pour about 3 oz (90 ml) of the same red into five glasses. One is your control, with nothing added.
  2. Glass A: steep a black tea bag for about 10 minutes, then remove it.6 B: squeeze in half a lemon. C: stir in a little sugar until it dissolves. D: add a small splash of vodka.
  3. Taste the control first. Then taste one glass, sip water, re-taste the control, and move on.
  4. For each glass, mark every dial as lower, the same or higher than the control. Then press "Check this glass".

No bottle this week? Do the quiz again in two or three days, then run the lab the next time you open a red.
